# Learning science behind the skills

**AI should power your learning, not replace it.** This repo is an educational demo. Every practice skill is attempt-first: you write your own answer before the AI shows options, an explanation, or an expert model. That rule comes from the main warning we cite about AI tutors: in Bastani et al. (PNAS 2025, PMCID PMC12232635), students who used an unguarded AI tutor did worse later when the AI was taken away, and guardrails reduced that harm. That evidence is mixed across settings, so treat it as a reason for caution, not a verdict. Anything you upload must follow your institution's AI policy; see [Responsible use](RESPONSIBLE-USE.md).

These skills are built around a few study methods that have research support: trying to answer before you see the answer, spacing your reviews, and explaining ideas in your own words. This page lists each skill, what it does, and the evidence behind it. Use it to judge how much weight each skill deserves in your own study plan.

## Evidence table

| Skill | What it does | Evidence | Strength note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/vignette` | Gives a clinical case and hides the answer options until you commit to an answer, then explains with feedback. | Testing with feedback improved clinical reasoning in a crossover randomised trial of test-enhanced learning [PMID:27295475]. In a small undergraduate study (four neuromuscular disorders), learning the mechanism behind a condition preserved diagnostic accuracy at 1 week; the authors call this suggestive, not proof [PMID:15612907]. Work on cognitive integration concerns tying basic science to clinical learning [PMID:27246965] [PMID:23969375]. | Strongest for the retrieval part. The integration studies are context only; they do not test this format. |
| `/teach-back` | You explain a concept in your own words first, then get feedback on gaps. | Self-explanation helped learners, and prompts plus worked examples improved it in an RCT [PMID:25626750]. Testing beat self-explanation in Year 1 medical students, though both helped [PMID:23746156]. Dunlosky rates self-explanation as moderate utility [PMID:26173288]. | Moderate. Pair it with testing rather than replacing testing. |
| Miss -> card -> review loop (and `/anki-sync`) | A missed question becomes a flashcard tagged `miss`, and `/anki-sync` sends it to Anki for spaced review. | Repeated testing beat restudy at 6 months in an RCT [PMID:19930508]. Spacing with short-answer retrieval and feedback helps [PMID:25920573]. A spacing meta-analysis supports distributing practice [PMID:16719566]. Cards seen in Anki predicted Step 1 scores, observational [PMID:26498443]. A 2026 systematic review found Anki associated with higher Step 1 scores and mixed results on course exams, observational [PMID:42183420]. | Retrieval and spacing evidence is strong. The Anki-to-exam link is observational, so it shows association, not cause. |
| `/flashcards` | Makes question-and-answer cards for retrieval practice. | Practice testing and distributed practice both rate high utility [PMID:26173288]. Repeated testing beat restudy at 6 months [PMID:19930508]. | Strong when you try to recall before flipping the card. |
| `/quiz` | Asks questions and gives feedback after you answer. | Testing beat self-explanation in Year 1 medical students [PMID:23746156]. Test-enhanced learning improved clinical reasoning in a crossover randomised trial [PMID:27295475]. Short-answer retrieval with feedback and spacing helps [PMID:25920573]. Interleaving meta-analysis found g=0.42, best for similar categories [PMID:31556629]. A chest X-ray study of mixed versus blocked practice found no difference [PMID:27072443]. | Strong for testing. Mixing topics is promising for similar categories but not consistent. |
| `/visualize` (concept maps) | Draws a concept map of how ideas connect. | A review of concept maps in learning [PMID:20374475]. Dunlosky rates imagery as low utility [PMID:26173288]. | Weaker. Use it to organize and to find gaps, then test yourself. |
| `/mnemonic-art` | Makes a memorable picture to hold a fact. | Dunlosky rates the keyword mnemonic and imagery as low utility [PMID:26173288]. People also misjudge which strategies work [PMID:23020639]. | Low evidence. A memory aid only, not a way to learn. |

## Why every skill makes you attempt first

Bastani et al. (PNAS 2025, PMCID PMC12232635) studied an AI tutor used without guardrails. Students who used it did worse later when they had to work without AI, and guardrails reduced that harm. That is why every skill here is attempt-first: you answer, explain, or recall before the AI shows you anything.

## Limits of this evidence

Anki evidence is observational: it shows that cards seen and exam scores go together, not that cards caused the scores. AI-harm evidence is mixed, so one study is not the whole picture and results depend on how the tool is used. A PMID existing is not the same as the paper supporting a claim; `scripts/check.py` confirms each PMID exists and is not retracted but cannot judge fit, so read the titles. Most of these studies did not use AI-generated questions, so their findings may not carry over fully. These skills are a study aid only and do not replace your curriculum, your instructors, or licensed resources.
