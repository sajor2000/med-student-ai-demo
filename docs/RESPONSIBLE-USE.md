# Responsible use

This project is an **educational demonstration**. It shows how medical students can use AI to
**power their learning, not replace it**. The AI drafts, cites, and quizzes; you do the
thinking.

## Use AI to learn, not to skip learning

- **Attempt first.** Answer every question and explain every mechanism yourself before you look
  at the AI's answer. Retrieval practice works only when you do the retrieving.
- **Open the sources.** A citation check shows a source *exists*, not that it *supports* the
  claim. Read the slide or paper behind anything you plan to rely on.
- **Expect mistakes.** AI can be confidently wrong. Your course materials and faculty are the
  ground truth for your exams.

## Your course materials belong to your institution

- Lecture slides, case guides, recordings, and question banks are the intellectual property of
  your medical school (or their authors). This repository grants no rights to them.
- Keep them in the gitignored `curriculum/` folder. Never commit, share, post, or redistribute
  them, including inside shared Anki decks.
- Everything in `sample/` and `examples/` is fictional and written for this repository.

## Follow your institution's AI policy

- Anything you give an AI tool leaves your computer. That includes Claude, Codex, OpenRouter
  image models, and the text of your PubMed and Paperclip searches.
- **Before you upload or paste any course content, confirm that your medical school's AI,
  data, and academic-integrity policies allow it,** and use only the tools and accounts your
  institution approves.
- If your school's policy and this repository disagree, **your school's policy wins.**

## Never

- Never enter patient information (names, dates, record numbers, images, or any detail from a
  real patient encounter).
- Never use these tools to complete graded assignments, quizzes, or exams.
- Never use the output for clinical decisions. This is a study aid, not medical advice.
