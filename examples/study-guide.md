## One-page summary
- Gram-positive cocci in clusters; **catalase +** (vs strep) and **coagulase +** (vs most other staph). [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S2]]
- It evades opsonization: **protein A** binds the IgG Fc region, and the **capsule** resists phagocytosis. [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S3]]
- Disease comes either from **invasion** (abscess, endocarditis, osteomyelitis) or from **toxins** (TSS, scalded skin, food poisoning). [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S4]] [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S5]]
- **MRSA** = *mecA* → **PBP2a** (low β-lactam affinity), carried on **SCCmec**. [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S6]] [PMID:30209034]

## Virulence factor → disease

## Integration thread (micro → immuno → path → pharm)
Protein A blocks antibody-mediated opsonization (immuno) [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S3]] → neutrophils pile up and wall off the
infection as an **abscess** (path) [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S5]] → treatment must work against PBP2a when MRSA is suspected (pharm). [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S6]]

## Self-test (answers below)
1. Why does catalase separate staph from strep?
2. Why would protein A make *S. aureus* harder to clear?
3. How does a superantigen activate so many T cells?
4. Why is MRSA resistant to almost all β-lactams?
5. Why is daptomycin avoided for pneumonia?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Staphylococci are catalase-positive and streptococci are not. [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S2]]
2. It binds the Fc region of IgG, blocking opsonization and phagocytosis. [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S3]]
3. It binds MHC II and the TCR outside the peptide groove, activating T cells regardless of antigen. [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S4]]
4. *mecA* encodes PBP2a, which has low affinity for β-lactams. [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S6]]
5. Pulmonary surfactant inactivates it. [[MICRO/Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)#S6]] [PMID:15898002]
</details>
