[P14] Case Presentation

[P15] Chief complaint

[P16] “There’s a painful bump on my arm that keeps getting bigger.”

[P17] History of present illness

[P18] Jordan Rivera is a 19-year-old college wrestler who presents to student health with a 4-day history of a red, swollen, painful lesion on the ulnar (medial) side of the right forearm. It began as what Jordan thought was a spider bite after practice. Over 48 hours it became firm and warm, and this morning it felt soft in the center. Jordan reports subjective fevers and fatigue. Two teammates had similar “boils” this season.

[P19] Past history

[P20] No chronic illnesses; no prior hospitalizations

[P21] Medications: none. Allergies: no known drug allergies

[P22] Social: shares mats and towels at practice; no injection drug use

[P23] Vital signs

[P24] Physical examination

[P25] A 4 cm erythematous, warm, tender nodule on the ulnar side of the right volar forearm with a fluctuant center and 2 cm of surrounding erythema. No lymphangitic streaking. Small, tender right epitrochlear node. The rest of the exam is normal.

[P26] Laboratory results

[P27] Reference ranges are typical adult values (hemoglobin and creatinine shown for an adult male); they vary by laboratory.

[P28] Procedure and microbiology

[P29] Bedside incision and drainage released 6 mL of thick yellow pus. Gram stain showed many neutrophils and gram-positive cocci in clusters. Culture grew large golden colonies with beta-hemolysis on blood agar.

[P30] Review the results above. Before reading on, write down: (1) the organism, (2) the host cells doing most of the work, and (3) what you would treat with and why.

[P53] Neutrophil Recruitment and Killing (LO-1102, LO-1103)

[P54] Recognition

[P55] Bacterial lipoproteins are the main ligands for TLR2 (with TLR1 or TLR6); whether lipoteichoic acid also signals through TLR2 is debated [PMID:18406368].

[P56] Tissue macrophages release IL-1, TNF-α, and IL-8 (CXCL8).

[P57] Recruitment (the leukocyte adhesion cascade)

[P58] Killing

[P59] Opsonized bacteria (IgG, C3b) are phagocytosed and the phagosome fuses with lysosomes.

[P60] NADPH oxidase produces superoxide (the respiratory burst); myeloperoxidase makes hypochlorite.

[P61] Pus is mostly dead neutrophils, bacteria, and tissue debris.

[P62] Clinical correlation

[P63] Chronic granulomatous disease (NADPH oxidase defect): recurrent S. aureus, Aspergillus, and other infections. Often taught as 'catalase-positive organisms', but in a mouse model catalase-negative S. aureus was equally virulent [PMID:11997027].

[P64] Leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1 (CD18 defect): little or no pus with marked neutrophilia; delayed umbilical cord separation is classic but not universal.

[P87] 2. A child has recurrent S. aureus abscesses and absent superoxide production on a nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) test. Which enzyme is defective?

[P88] A. Myeloperoxidase

[P89] B. NADPH oxidase

[P90] C. Superoxide dismutase

[P91] D. Catalase

[P92] E. Lysozyme

[P93] Answer: B

[P94] No superoxide on NBT = absent respiratory burst = chronic granulomatous disease (NADPH oxidase defect). Myeloperoxidase deficiency spares superoxide production.

[P116] Staphylococcus aureus (LO-1201, LO-1202, LO-1203)

[P117] Methicillin resistance

[P118] mecA (on SCCmec) encodes PBP2a, a penicillin-binding protein with low affinity for beta-lactams.

[P119] MRSA is resistant to nearly all beta-lactams (exception: anti-MRSA cephalosporins such as ceftaroline).

[P120] Community-associated strains often carry PVL (its role in virulence is debated) and cause skin abscesses in healthy young people, especially with shared equipment and skin contact (athletes, dormitories) [PMID:17146447][PMID:22759601].
