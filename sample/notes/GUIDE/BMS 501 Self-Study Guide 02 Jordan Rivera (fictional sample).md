[P15] Chief complaint

[P16] “There’s a painful bump on my arm that keeps getting bigger.”

[P17] History of present illness

[P18] Jordan Rivera is a 19-year-old college wrestler who presents to student health with a 4-day history of a red, swollen, painful lesion on the right forearm. It began as what Jordan thought was a spider bite after practice. Over 48 hours it became firm and warm, and this morning it felt soft in the center. Jordan reports subjective fevers and fatigue. Two teammates had similar “boils” this season.

[P19] Past history

[P20] No chronic illnesses; no prior hospitalizations

[P21] Medications: none. Allergies: no known drug allergies

[P22] Social: shares mats and towels at practice; no injection drug use

[P23] Vital signs

[P24] Physical examination

[P25] A 4 cm erythematous, warm, tender nodule on the right volar forearm with a fluctuant center and 2 cm of surrounding erythema. No lymphangitic streaking. Small, tender right epitrochlear node. The rest of the exam is normal.

[P26] Laboratory results

[P27] Procedure and microbiology

[P28] Bedside incision and drainage released 6 mL of thick yellow pus. Gram stain showed many neutrophils and gram-positive cocci in clusters. Culture grew large golden colonies with beta-hemolysis on blood agar.

[P29] Review the results above. Before reading on, write down: (1) the organism, (2) the host cells doing most of the work, and (3) what you would treat with and why.

## [P52] Neutrophil Recruitment and Killing (LO-1102, LO-1103)

[P53] Recognition

[P54] Bacterial lipoproteins are the main ligands for TLR2 (with TLR1 or TLR6); whether lipoteichoic acid also signals through TLR2 is debated.

[P55] Tissue macrophages release IL-1, TNF-α, and IL-8 (CXCL8).

[P56] Recruitment (the leukocyte adhesion cascade)

[P57] Killing

[P58] Opsonized bacteria (IgG, C3b) are phagocytosed and the phagosome fuses with lysosomes.

[P59] NADPH oxidase produces superoxide (the respiratory burst); myeloperoxidase makes hypochlorite.

[P60] Pus is mostly dead neutrophils, bacteria, and tissue debris.

[P61] Clinical correlation

[P62] Chronic granulomatous disease (NADPH oxidase defect): recurrent S. aureus, Aspergillus, and other infections. Often taught as 'catalase-positive organisms', but in a mouse model catalase-negative S. aureus was equally virulent [PMID:11997027].

[P63] Leukocyte adhesion deficiency type 1 (CD18 defect): no pus, delayed umbilical cord separation.

## [P115] Staphylococcus aureus (LO-1201, LO-1202, LO-1203)

[P116] Methicillin resistance

[P117] mecA (on SCCmec) encodes PBP2a, a penicillin-binding protein with low affinity for beta-lactams.

[P118] MRSA is resistant to nearly all beta-lactams (exception: ceftaroline).

[P119] Community-associated strains often carry PVL and cause skin abscesses in healthy young people, especially with shared equipment and skin contact (athletes, dormitories).
