## [S1] Staphylococcus aureus (sample lecture)
- Synthetic demo lecture for med-student-ai-demo
- Learning objectives: (1) identify S. aureus in the lab; (2) link virulence factors to disease; (3) explain methicillin resistance

## [S2] Lab identification
- Gram-positive cocci in clusters
- Catalase positive (separates staphylococci from streptococci)
- Coagulase positive (separates S. aureus from most other staphylococci)
- Beta-hemolytic, golden colonies on blood agar

## [S3] Surface virulence factors
- Protein A binds the Fc region of IgG, blocking opsonization and phagocytosis
- Capsule (polysaccharide) resists phagocytosis
- Clumping factor binds fibrinogen

## [S4] Toxins and toxin-mediated disease
- TSST-1 is a superantigen: cross-links MHC II and T-cell receptors outside the peptide groove, causing massive cytokine release (toxic shock syndrome)
- Exfoliative toxins cleave desmoglein-1, causing staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome
- Preformed heat-stable enterotoxins cause rapid-onset vomiting (food poisoning, 1-6 h)
- Panton-Valentine leukocidin (PVL) kills leukocytes; associated with necrotizing skin infection and pneumonia

## [S5] Clinical spectrum
- Skin and soft tissue: impetigo, furuncles, abscesses, cellulitis
- Invasive: bacteremia, endocarditis (often tricuspid valve in people who inject drugs), osteomyelitis, septic arthritis
- Abscess formation is driven by neutrophil recruitment

## [S6] Methicillin resistance (MRSA)
- mecA gene encodes PBP2a, a penicillin-binding protein with low affinity for beta-lactams
- mecA is carried on a mobile element, SCCmec
- Result: resistance to almost all beta-lactams
- Options for serious MRSA infection include vancomycin or daptomycin (daptomycin not for pneumonia: inactivated by surfactant)
